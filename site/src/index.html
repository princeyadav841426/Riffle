The problem

Quick Look stops at the folder.

Press Space on a file and macOS shows you the file. Press Space on a folder and you get its icon. To see what's inside, you open it, look, close it, and go back. Riffle replaces that whole round trip with one keypress.

Who it's for

Made for people who live in folders.

We're a video studio. Our days are card dumps, export folders and asset packs, and Riffle is the tool we wanted for them.

Video editors

Find the take without digging through the card dump.

Press Space on 01 Raw Footage. Every clip is listed with its size and date. Click one and you see a frame from it.

Designers

Check an asset folder before you drag it in.

Click any image and its pixel dimensions appear beside it. Catch the 800-pixel logo before it lands in a 4K export.

Developers & writers

Read the README, not its syntax.

Press Space on a Markdown file and get headings, tables and code blocks, rendered on your Mac instead of raw # and **.

Anyone with a full disk

Find what's eating your storage.

Every subfolder shows its real size, measured all the way down. Sort by Size and the heaviest folder is at the top.

Features

Everything Quick Look should have shown you.

Real folder sizes

Every folder is measured recursively in the background. The numbers match the "on disk" size in Get Info.

Expand in place

Open subfolders right inside the preview with the disclosure arrow. No new windows.

Thumbnails

Photos, video frames, PDFs and documents preview in the side pane as you click through.

Details that fit

Kind, size, dimensions, item count, modified and created dates. Long file names wrap instead of getting cut off.

Filters and sorting

Show only Folders, Images, Docs or Media. Sort by name, date, size or kind.

Markdown, formatted

Tables, code blocks and quotes render on your Mac. Scripts inside documents never run.

A divider that drags

Make the preview pane as wide as you like. Riffle remembers where you left it.

Private by design

Read-only, sandboxed by macOS. No account, no analytics, nothing sent anywhere. The code is public.

Free, for good

No trial, no Pro tier, no in-app purchase. MIT-licensed, so anyone can read, fork or improve it.

How it works

Two minutes to install. Then it's just Space.

Download

Get the free .dmg ({{dmgSize}}), open it, and drag Riffle into your Applications folder.

Download Riffle

Open it once

Riffle isn't sold through Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm it the first time. Click Done, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Or paste this into Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Riffle.app

Press Space

Riffle shows a green check when it's ready. Now select any folder in Finder and press Space. Riffle runs inside Quick Look, so there's no app to keep open.

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Is Riffle really free?
Yes. There's no trial, no Pro version and no in-app purchase. Riffle is open source under the MIT license, so the code is public on GitHub and anyone can build it themselves.
Why does macOS say it can't verify Riffle?
Apple shows that message for apps that aren't distributed through the App Store or notarized with a paid developer account. Riffle is free and open source, so it skips that. Click Done, then open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.
Does Riffle read or upload my files?
Riffle runs inside Apple's Quick Look sandbox with read-only access to what you preview. It has no account, no analytics and no tracking, and it doesn't send your files anywhere. You can check every line of the code on GitHub.
Which Macs does it work on?
Macs running macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Riffle is a universal app, so it runs natively on Apple silicon and on Intel Macs.
Can Riffle preview ZIP or RAR files?
Not yet. If you need to look inside archives without unpacking them, Folder Preview by Anybox does that well.
I press Space and still see the blue folder icon. What now?
Open Riffle from Applications and check for the green tick. If it says Riffle is off, click Extension Settings and switch Riffle on under Quick Look. If you have another folder previewer installed, such as PeekX, remove it, because only one app can answer the same Space press.
How do I uninstall it?
Drag Riffle from Applications to the Trash. Quick Look goes back to showing folder icons.
Who makes Riffle?
Aignix, a video studio. We built Riffle for our own editing folders and give it away. It started as a fork of PeekX by ALTIC, an MIT-licensed project, and has since gained a rebuilt preview pane, thumbnails, details and offline Markdown.
